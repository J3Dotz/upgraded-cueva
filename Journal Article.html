Arriving in autumn: the best time most guests overlook.

By Adrian · November 2025 · 6 min read

Most of our guests arrive in July and August, and most of them, by the end of their stay, ask the same question: when should we come back? The honest answer is: not in summer.

October is when the property settles into its best self. The light gets longer in the afternoon and stays low for hours instead of minutes. The valley empties out. The kitchen, which spends July chasing tomatoes and basil, finally has time to slow down and cook the things we actually want to feed people. None of this is a secret. It is just that most guests stop looking past the calendar of school holidays, and so they miss it.

The light, and what it does to the day.

By the second week of October the sun no longer sits directly above the masia at lunchtime. It comes in at an angle, low across the valley, and the cream walls of the south terrace turn a soft amber for the last two hours of the afternoon. We do most of our long lunches outside in October, often into early November. The air is warm, the light is on your side, and it is the kind of weather that makes guests cancel whatever they had planned for the rest of the day.

The walking is also better. Desert de les Palmes, fifteen minutes from the property, becomes properly usable again — in July the cork oaks are baking and the routes are too exposed for most people. In October you can leave after breakfast and be back for a swim by lunch.

"July is when the coast is loud. October is when the inland actually lets you in."

— something we tell guests, often

What the kitchen does once it stops sweating.

Summer at the lodge is, frankly, a kitchen full of tomatoes. We are not complaining. But by mid-September the produce list at the market in Cabanes changes character: pumpkin, the first wild mushrooms from the Sierra, late figs, fresh almonds, and — by the end of October — the first oranges from the valley behind us. Our cook stops cooking quick and starts cooking long: slow lamb, rice with bone broth, escalivada that has actually been allowed to rest.

It is also when we run our wine and olive tastings most often. The local producers are pressing in October and November and they tend to be in a generous mood. If you have ever wanted to taste an oil within a fortnight of the olives leaving the tree, this is the only month of the year you can do it without booking a flight a year in advance.

Where the coast goes when the crowds leave.

The Costa Azahar in August is a wonderful, busy, slightly exhausting strip of coastline. In October it becomes one of the quietest beach drives in Spain. The water is still warm enough to swim into the first week of November in a good year. Peñíscola, forty-five minutes north of us, is genuinely walkable again. Benicàssim has its beach to itself by 4pm. We send guests down for an afternoon and they come back wondering where the rest of the country went.

If you are thinking about it: aim for the second half of October, give yourself four nights rather than two, and let us know if you want the kitchen to lean into something specific — a mushroom dinner, a pressing visit, a long table on the lower terrace. We will arrange it. October is the month for the small extra things.

The summer guests have a wonderful time. The October guests have a slightly different one. We have a soft preference, which we are not really supposed to admit.
